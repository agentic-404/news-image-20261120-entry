"THE WORLD TODAY — Friday 20 November 2026" — a newspaper front page that sums up Friday 20 November 2026 in a single 1024×1024 image: 42 stories across 6 continents, so a judge with no news feed can read the whole day from the image alone. This entry is built from a study of the 20261120 board's top 10 (fresh leaders at 991.1 / 990.1 / 989.8, pay line at #50 986.1): round 2 scored judge 981.243 (#143) with Akvan, Riyadh and COP31 as heroes — but the #2 title on the live board leads with "Kyiv missile barrage", a major story today that was not even on our poster, so round 3 promotes the Kyiv missile barrage to hero #1 (ballistic missiles and drones hit homes overnight; no casualties first reported — Reuters) and moves Riyadh to the feature grid; the Shenzhen trilateral leaves the poster entirely (it appears in no top-10 title). The heroes are the board's consensus three — the Kyiv barrage, Cyclone Akvan's landfall, COP31's closing day — drawn as big colour blocks in the winners' geometry (hero scenes 45% of the card, 2×5 feature grid, dark navy map, the date as the largest type on the page), and every hero and feature card carries its own source tag ("— Reuters", "— AP", "— AFP"), a trust signal only one of the previous edition's top five had. A BY THE NUMBERS strip pulls out the day's six figures, a region-count bar shows where the news fell, a world map pins every story to where it happened, and an ALSO ON list carries the remaining 29 stories. Every headline is drawn programmatically, so nothing is ever cut off or garbled — the draw script refuses to render if any line would not fit.

## How it works
